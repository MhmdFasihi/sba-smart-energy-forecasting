# Smart Energy Forecasting: results report

_Generated by `src/final_eval.py` from `results/test_results.json`; checked by `src/check_report.py`._

Task: **day-ahead** forecast of the German day-ahead electricity price (ct/kWh) and solar generation (MWh per hour) for day D+1 (00:00-23:00), made at 12:00 of day D (auction cut-off, 12-36 h ahead), with a low (10 %) / middle (50 %) / high (90 %) band, from the last 168 h before 12:00 of day D. Data: SMARD (Bundesnetzagentur | SMARD.de, CC BY 4.0); the price is built from the native 15-minute day-ahead prices (hourly mean); weather from DWD observations (Open-Meteo for the last weeks, same time convention). Split: train 2019-2024, validation 2025, test 2026-01-01 to 2026-11-16 (320 forecast days). Each model is trained with 3 seeds (mean ± std over seeds). Two input families are reported: **[noon-safe]** = calendar + the day-ahead load forecast of the target day as known-future inputs (the primary result), and **[all fc]** = additionally the official day-ahead wind and solar forecasts (an upper bound, see the timing caveat).

## Protocol and model selection (validation only)
- Input ablation (seed 0, mean validation pinball loss over LSTM/CNN/PatchTST): base 0.0841; + weather 0.0850; + known-future inputs 0.0437; + weather + future 0.0437; + within-hour 15-min price range 0.0429. Decision: known-future inputs (official SMARD day-ahead solar / wind / load forecasts and calendar of the target day) used; 15-min range feature used.
- Timing check (added later, seed 0, same hyper-parameters): with only the noon-safe known inputs (calendar + load forecast) the validation loss is 0.0872, i.e. about the same as without any known-future input (0.0841) and far above the 0.0429 with the wind/solar forecasts. The whole gain of the known-future inputs therefore comes from the official wind and solar forecasts, whose vintage at 12:00 is not verified (see Limitations). Rule fixed before the strict models were trained: the noon-safe models are the primary result, the all-forecast models an upper bound.
- Hyper-parameter grid (learning rate × dropout, validation loss, seed 0): LSTM: lr 0.001, dropout 0.1; CNN: lr 0.001, dropout 0.2; PatchTST: lr 0.0003, dropout 0.1.
- Selection (primary, noon-safe family): lowest mean validation pinball loss over seeds → **LSTM [noon-safe]** (0.0818 ± 0.0015); next: PatchTST [noon-safe] 0.0824, CNN [noon-safe] 0.0976. Best of the all-forecast family (upper bound): PatchTST [all fc] (0.0420 ± 0.0008). The test set was scored once afterwards (`final_eval.py` refuses to run twice without `--force`) and was not used for any choice. The app default is the best seed of the winner (`lstm_strict_intra_final_s1`).

After recalibration the test coverage is price 0.53-0.74, solar 0.68-0.78 (target 0.80). Price coverage rises for 9 of 9 models and solar coverage for 7 of 9; pinball loss is lower for 17 of 18 model/target pairs. The price bands remain clearly too narrow on 2026. A likely reason is that 2026 prices are less like 2025 than a constant widening can absorb (distribution shift); this was not investigated further.

## Deviations from the guide
- Day-ahead protocol (12:00 cut-off) instead of forecasting from midnight; known-future inputs added (see ablation); the primary result uses only the inputs verifiably available at noon (calendar + load forecast).
- PatchTST is a native PyTorch implementation, because the neuralforecast version is univariate (no exogenous inputs).
- CNN uses kernel 4 (dilations 1-32, receptive field 190 h) so the last step sees the whole 168 h week, 6 layers as in the guide but **128 filters** (not the guide's 64): all three models share one width setting (128 = LSTM units / CNN filters / PatchTST d_model). LSTM: 2 layers, 128 units as in the guide.
- Training: Adam, batch 64, up to 50 epochs, early stopping after 5 epochs, as in the guide; the learning rate and dropout were chosen on validation from the grid above (so the learning rate is not always 0.001). Trained on a Mac (Apple MPS), not Google Colab.
- Libraries: charts in the app use Altair (Streamlit built-in) instead of plotly; neuralforecast is not used (see above).
- The LLM explanation uses Anthropic or NVIDIA (optional), with a template fallback without API key.

## Limitations
- 3 seeds and one split; differences of a few percent between models are within noise.
- Weather was trained on observations, not forecasts, so the solar gain would be smaller in real use (guide section 9). The last weeks of weather come from Open-Meteo (modelled values), not DWD.
- Timing of the official forecasts (SMARD user manual 04/2026): the day-ahead load forecast must be delivered two hours before the day-ahead exchange closes (before 12:00) and is only revised for changes of 10 % or more; the day-ahead wind and solar forecasts must be delivered 'by 18:00 of the previous day'. SMARD stores one value per hour, not the vintage available at 12:00 of day D, and this project has no archive of those vintages. A wind/solar forecast delivered after 12:00 would not exist in a real noon run, so the [all fc] models are an upper bound for the noon protocol. The [noon-safe] models avoid the problem but, on the test year, do not beat the naive baselines: the skill of this project depends on the wind and solar forecasts.
- Without weather forecasts as inputs, solar and the wind-driven part of the price cannot be predicted well for tomorrow (weather in this project is observed, not forecast). Archived weather forecasts for the whole training period would be the next step to get a noon-safe model with skill; they were not used here.
- The 'naive day D (price only)' baseline was added after the test set had been scored once (it needs no tuning and changes no model choice); `final_eval.py` was re-run with `--force` only to add it and to correct report wording. All model predictions are unchanged.
- Negative prices and 2022 produce large errors. Costs in the planner use wholesale prices only (no taxes or grid fees).
